01 · The shape of the service

One frozen lane, one front door, a host that never sleeps on power

The judges converged on an appliance, not a dev box. The iGPU runs exactly one vLLM engine. Everything around it exists to prove the engine is healthy, to recover without you, and to say so. Click a block for the reasoning.

Clientsanywhere
Why the phone sees a sentinel, not a model. Every alert path that depends on the LLM dies with the LLM. The ops bot is plain Python, posts a ready line every morning whether or not anything is wrong, and its buttons run polkit-gated systemd actions. Silence then means "box dead", which the Spark's dead-man check turns into a message.
Why mosh and tmux. Plain SSH dies with the WiFi after sleep. mosh roams across address changes, tmux under a lingering user session keeps the shell alive, and never-suspend-on-AC means the session is still there in the morning. The harness runs on the laptop; the Claw is a token endpoint.
Tailscale over wg0 · wg0 ordered after network-online
Always-on home nodeone DGX Spark, on the LAN
Why the Spark holds the things that must outlive the Claw. A dead-man check on the same box as the service cannot report the box dying. Bot memory is the only irreplaceable data, so it is versioned hourly and backed up off the console. Quantization and BF16 reference runs evict the serving model on the Claw and leak Level Zero memory on every failed launch, so they move to the Spark. The judges also rescued a reversal: run the gateway on the Spark and make the Claw a pure endpoint, which removes the bots' dependence on a device with documented sleep and WiFi outages.
Tailscale · fallback route for the bots when the Claw is down
The consoleMSI Claw 8 AI+ · Nobara 43 · never suspends on AC · reboots at 04:35 when idle
Why a nightly reboot. Crashed and even gracefully stopped vLLM launches leak Level Zero shared memory on this driver, and a reboot is the only documented reset. The timer fires only on AC, when no request is running and no one is logged in over SSH. Never suspending on AC removes the WiFi-after-sleep failure entirely instead of repairing it. The watchdog's repair script reloads whichever op-mode actually holds iwlwifi: the skeptics found your existing resume unit reloads iwlmvm, which is probably a silent no-op on the iwlmld kernels the BE201 uses.
Why llama-swap in front of vLLM. Harnesses, bots and scripts all point at one URL that never changes. Model switching becomes an alias, swaps happen only when a request names another model, and the GGUF fallback can take the same port when vLLM is down. The Quadlet unit pins the image by digest, never pulls on its own, never auto-restarts (a restart loop burns the Level Zero pool), and runs a preflight as an ExecCondition so a refusal is a skip, not a failure. A ready service polls health, sends a one-token completion and checks the startup log for the right attention backend before the gateway is allowed to depend on it.
Why the bots are the fragile tenant. OpenClaw's structural fit for two personas is still the best, but it is high churn with a security history, so it runs sandboxed with podman, an exec allowlist and secret references instead of plaintext tokens. Its workspace is the only irreplaceable state on the device, so it is a git repo with hourly commits pushed to the Spark. The nanobot bake-off decides whether every OpenClaw-specific hook gets thrown away.
Why alerting is positive. Failure-only alerts cannot distinguish "all green" from "WiFi dead". The ready-check posts every morning, the Spark expects its heartbeat, and the OnFailure notifier is twenty lines of curl routed outside wg0. Crash bundles capture journal, dmesg for xe and iwlwifi, and meminfo, so a morning incident can be triaged from the phone.
Why the NPU is empty in version one. Every NPU idea assumes a user-mode driver that is not packaged for Nobara and an OpenVINO runtime whose memory and suspend behaviour on this device nobody has measured. A one-day hardware-truth sprint (mic capture, driver, a Whisper smoke test) decides the whole family of ideas; until it passes, the NPU stays out of the critical path.

02 · Boot, then prove it

Nothing depends on a unit that has not passed its own test

The sequence matters: each step is a gate for the next. A refusal at step 2 is a condition skip with a bounded retry timer, never a restart loop.

1
Network comes up in the right order

wg-quick@wg0 is ordered after network-online.target with an interface-agnostic PostUp route; tailscaled follows. The documented boot race in your OpenClaw setup guide is otherwise reproduced on every unattended reboot.

2
Preflight before the engine

llama-swap preloads the default profile. The vllm@stable unit runs an ExecCondition: drop caches, replicate the driver's usable-memory formula from /proc/meminfo with no torch import, compare Shmem and MemAvailable against the post-boot baseline, and exit 75 if free memory is under weights plus KV plus about 2 GiB. A skip arms a 2 to 5 minute retry timer with a bounded counter.

3
Ready means proven

vllm-ready polls /health, sends one max_tokens=1 completion and checks the startup log against a small yaml: FLASH_ATTN backend in use, graph mode NONE, no "falling back to PyTorch reference attention", prefix caching off on any GDN plus MTP profile. The gateway unit Requires it.

4
Bots start and warm the cache

OpenClaw (later nanobot) starts; a oneshot sends one real-prefix request per harness so the first user turn does not pay the full prefill. OpenClaw's heartbeat keeps the prefix warm afterwards.

5
Say so, every morning

At 07:15 the ready-check posts a positive line: model resident, probe latency, bots polling. The Spark's dead-man receives its heartbeat. No message is the alarm.

6
Reset while nobody is looking

At 04:35, if on AC, idle for 30 minutes, with no remote SSH session, and either a leak flag is set or the day count says so: graceful stop, then reboot. This is the honest backstop for the Level Zero shared-memory leak until experiment 1 proves it reclaimable.

06 · Disagreements

What the judges rescued, and what they struck

Skeptics graded ideas as written. Judges graded the reduced variant and sometimes overruled.

Never sleep on AC, nightly reboot

S39 · skeptic low · judges 8.5 / 8.5 / 4.0

The value skeptic called it low because ninety percent of the idea is a fifteen-minute config change. The MVP and Reliability judges rank those fifteen minutes first overall: they remove the WiFi-after-sleep trigger and are the only documented reset for the shared-memory leak. The Delight judge objects that it fights the handheld use. Resolution: ship the core, drop the battery choreography.

Gateway on a Spark

S33 · skeptic low · rescued to 5.5 to 6.0

The skeptic killed the leader-election and sync-lock mechanism as a flapping machine. All three judges rescued the reversal: run the gateway on an always-on Spark and make the Claw a pure endpoint. Half a day, no new code, and it removes the bots' dependence on a device with documented sleep, WiFi and Windows outages. The cost is no bots offline while travelling unless you toggle it.

Preflight

S05 · skeptic medium · judges 8.0 / 7.0 / 2.5

The skeptic saw a consolidation of rules already written down. Two judges rate it high because the no-restart-loop detail prevents the one self-inflicted failure that forces reboots. Build it inside the Quadlet unit, not as its own project.

The Delight judge's handheld slate

S24 · S27 · S26 · S21 · S29 · S28 · skeptic low · Delight 5.5 to 8.0

Paddle push-to-talk, an appliance face in Gaming Mode, the Claw as an MCP body for bigger brains, an NPU dial tone, quiet decode, fingerprint approvals. The argument: a Spark beats the Claw at every token and the laptop at every keyboard, so the handheld earns its service only if the service uses the screen, paddles, mics, battery and mobility. The other judges accept every feasibility cut and park these in "this quarter" behind the hardware-truth sprint.

Probes and canary, minus the chaos drill

S40 · struck in part

All agree on the probes, the golden canary and the log antibodies. The Reliability judge struck the chaos drill outright: kill -9, rtcwake and WiFi drops are the three faults this hardware cannot self-heal, so rehearsing them is rehearsing an outage.

Watchdog: smaller and more urgent

S38 · judges 7.5 / 7.0 / 4.5

Agreement on the ladder. The Reliability judge adds that iTCO almost certainly does not exist on Lunar Lake, and that the iwlmvm reload in your existing resume unit is probably a no-op on iwlmld kernels. The fix is a few lines, and it is more urgent than the idea's framing.

Facade versus llama-swap

S01 vs S04

The skeptic treated the health-routing facade and llama-swap as peers. The MVP judge would not build the facade's 500 to 800 lines of SSE proxy until llama-swap is in place and a measured gap appears.

Clock cap

S29 · skeptic low

Decode is bandwidth-bound, so capping the GPU clock might buy quiet for little speed. MVP keeps it as a zero-code half-day measurement, Delight raises it for fanless nights, Reliability warns a cap doubles time-to-first-token on the long prompts the bots send at night. Measure once, ship a profile only if a knee exists.

The eight unverified ideas

T11 to T18 · no skeptic pass

Judged directly on the same standard. T11, bot memory in git with restic to a Spark, lands in the top two across all judges. When not to upgrade

SKIP

The default. No tracked watchlist issue has closed and no CVE touches the pinned stack. The frozen stable lane is the product. Watchlist: your two UMA PRs, prefix caching with MTP on GDN, the graph-mode logits bug, the replay hang, the n-gram assert, the kernels' spec-metadata issue.

CANDIDATE

One watchlist item closed. Schedule an experimental-lane run in a daytime idle window, never a nightly lane swap on this hardware. Promotion needs three green gate runs of the same digest on three distinct days plus one release-tier run, never in a week tagged busy, at most once every six weeks, and always by a human /promote that only retags.

FORCED

A CVE or a driver end-of-life. Still through the gates, still with :stable-prev kept for rollback.

09 · Open questions

What nobody could answer from a desk

  1. Is the Level Zero shared-memory leak real on v0.32? Finding 3 was measured by hand on v0.19. Nobody has characterized the residue (tmpfs, TTM pool or driver) under systemd's control-group kill mode. Experiment 1 decides whether the nightly reboot is a backstop or a crutch.
  2. Does S0ix preserve the Level Zero context? Decides whether battery suspend is ever a usable idle state or always a planned stop.
  3. Does /dev/watchdog exist on this firmware? iTCO has no Lunar Lake entry; an ACPI WDAT table might. Decides how much of the watchdog idea is hardware.
  4. Which op-mode does the kernel bind for the BE201? If iwlmld, your existing wifi-resume unit has been a no-op and the D3cold workaround is doing all the work.
  5. Does the mic array capture under Nobara, and does libfprint know the sensor? Two five-minute checks that gate a quarter of the catalogue.
  6. How is the Linux partition laid out, and is snapper configured? Decides whether the disk-layout ideas matter and whether the swapfile must move before snapshots.
  7. Can the Sparks suspend and wake on LAN? No official statement. Decides whether any Spark "herding" idea is possible or the Sparks simply stay on.
  8. What does OpenClaw put in the system prompt per turn, and does its Telegram adapter expose a Bot API base URL? Its docs were unreachable in this run. Decides the prefix-cache hit rate and two gap-round ideas.
  9. Does the work laptop allow mosh, Tailscale and agent forwarding, and does it have Thunderbolt 4? Decides the remote-session and umbilical ideas.
  10. Will the gateway move to a Spark or stay on the Claw? This one decision retires most of the on-console reliability ideas for the bots and changes where secrets, backups and the dead-man live. Settle the nanobot bake-off first.
  11. Why does XPU weight ingest run at about 0.6 GiB/s from a warm page cache? The one load-time question with real upside, and an upstream item rather than a local patch.
10 · Method and caveats

How to read the numbers

Who said what

  • Sixteen ideator agents, one technique each: SCAMPER, Six Thinking Hats, reverse brainstorming, first principles from the hardware, analogical transfer, jobs-to-be-done, morphological analysis, TRIZ, starbursting, worst possible idea, steal from the pros, future-backwards with a pre-mortem, lean value-stream mapping, constraint relaxation, Crazy 8s, biomimicry.
  • A clustering editor merged 306 raw ideas into 54. A completeness critic named four blind spots; four more ideators added 47 raw ideas, clustered into 18.
  • Two skeptic agents per idea, prompted to refute: one on feasibility for this console, one on value against what is already decided or already exists. Web lookups are marked in the verdicts; several documentation sites were unreachable from the sandbox.
  • Three judge agents with declared angles scored every survivor 0 to 10 and assigned a horizon. The consensus score is the mean of the available judge scores.

What to trust less

  • All scores are from agents, not from measurements on your device. Memory and speed numbers come from your own earlier findings; everything marked est. is an estimate.
  • Eight gap-round ideas (T11 to T18) never received skeptic verdicts because the run hit usage limits five times across six days. The judges scored them directly.
  • No idea was judged feasible as written. The catalogue describes the reduced variants the skeptics would accept, and the judges scored those.
  • The two rejected ideas are shown with their reasons under the Rejected filter.
